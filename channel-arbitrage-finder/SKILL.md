# Channel Arbitrage Finder

Research requires live web search and access to public video or channel evidence.

Help a creator decide which repeatable video format to test this month. The essential signal is **repeated breakouts by small accounts in one specific format with limited effective supply**. Follow the original sequence in [the Method Brief](references/method.md), sections 1–3. Section 9 identifies additions A1–A6. Call a result a hypothesis until its evidence supports a stronger conclusion.

## 1. Interview and confirm

Open with “Let’s find a video format you can make consistently and give viewers a reason to choose.” Follow [the intake script](references/intake-questions.md) in groups of 2–4 questions. Wait for each answer. Ask the production-mode question explicitly, including when returning to a saved profile. For a returning user, show their current mode as the proposed choice and ask what changed. Use their existing niche first. Respect topics they rule out.

Show one screen of decisions, let the user correct it and receive confirmation. Save the confirmed answers using [the profile template](templates/user-profile.md) in the user’s working area as `channel-profile.md`. If the host cannot write files, provide the completed profile as a single copyable Markdown block or downloadable file. Reuse it on later runs. Keep research results outside the reusable skill folder.

## 2. Research live

Follow [the research playbook](references/research-playbook.md). Generate 10–20 distinct niche × repeatable format × angle candidates. Search each candidate and each selected platform. Establish the target market, document demand and supply, check repeated small-account outliers, then choose the relevant strategy. Test any niche the user brought first. For a transferred format, keep origin and destination evidence separate. If it would require a creator program, price and staff the actual work before proposing it.

Use live, dated sources. Distinguish an opened original page, a dated indexed observation, a creator's own claim, a calculation and a hypothesis. Include contrary examples, hidden counts and limits of search coverage. If live search is unavailable, say so and stop the research run. If a specific platform is inaccessible, document the attempt, work through other legitimate public sources, and mark unsupported fields **unverified**.

## 3. Select the opportunities

Use [the scoring rubric](references/scoring-rubric.md). Show all seven 1–10 scores and their weighted total, plus evidence status. Remove hard mismatches and explain every dropped candidate in a sentence. Choose up to three eligible options. If fewer than three qualify, use remaining viable slots for clearly labelled pilot hypotheses. If hard exclusions leave fewer than three feasible concepts, report the actual number. A high fit score cannot upgrade missing market evidence.

## 4. Design and deliver

Use [the production guide](references/production-modes.md) for the confirmed mode and [the art direction library](references/art-styles.md) when selecting a visual treatment. Keep the viewer payoff from a working format. Develop original scripts, titles, visual identity and material. Price actual subscriptions, credit limits, production hours and planned uploads before calling a plan feasible.

Complete [the opportunity report](templates/opportunity-report.md) for each finalist. Write the first 15 seconds so the creator can record them. Trace adaptations to specific observed outliers. Mark original hypotheses and unverified comparators accurately. Use source links with check dates for external claims and identify creative proposals, sample scenarios and estimates. Give the full plan in the report and keep chat turns brief. End with exactly one question inviting the user to choose an opportunity. If they pick one, continue with their choice of 30 titles, three complete scripts or a full brand kit.
