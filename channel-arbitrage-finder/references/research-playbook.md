# Research playbook

**Video basis:** Method Brief §§1–3, 6 and 8. **Author additions A2, A3, A4 and A6:** the collection protocol, screening defaults, policy checks and test design below. A “format” is a promise with a repeatable sequence and payoff. “History Shorts” describes a subject and a surface. “Give the viewer one clue from an obsolete machine, let them guess its job, then show the mechanism” describes a format.

## Prepare the search

1. Read the confirmed profile. Define the target viewer, viewing language, region, platform and surface for each candidate. Start with any proposed niche. Generate 10–20 rows. Cross expertise with mechanics such as timed identification, visible A/B test or constraint-led walkthrough. Each row gets a distinct episode sequence, viewer payoff and one way a creator could improve the available work.
2. Record today's check date and the exact start of the search window. Default to the last 90 days to balance freshness and sample size. When a viable low-volume market is sparse, search the previous 90 days separately or the same period last year for a seasonal topic. Historical matches establish precedent and cannot count as current breakouts.
3. Use the relevant local language, spelling and creator vocabulary. Build queries from **object + action/payoff**, then vary one phrase at a time. Use web search and platform search where available. For example, for a history pilot search YouTube for `punch card how computer reads holes`, `punch card reader demonstration`, and the same queries with a date filter. For a coaching pilot try `dumbbell goblet squat side by side setup` and `goblet squat two setups comparison`. A search for “best niches” rarely yields comparable posts.
4. For every candidate and chosen surface, inspect recent results and a relevance-sorted sample. Try two materially different searches, with and without the format label, before recording no observable match. Where available use Videos, Shorts, Latest or upload-date filters. Web fallbacks include `site:youtube.com/watch "punch card" "reader"`, `site:youtube.com/shorts "punch card"`, `site:tiktok.com "goblet squat" "setup"` and `site:instagram.com/reel "goblet squat" "setup"`. Obtain URLs and open originals. Record terms, filters, locale, check date, inspected count and blocked surfaces.

## Run Victor’s test in order

**1 · Define the market (00:33–01:48).** Write one sentence with the target viewer, repeatable sequence, payoff and nearest substitute. Label each result exact format / adjacent / unrelated. Compare like with like on platform, surface, language and time since upload. One viral topic, series name, art style or hashtag cannot establish a format.

**2 · Test whether demand outruns effective supply (01:48–02:13).** Look for repeat viewership of the payoff. Inspect ordinary uploads as well as hits. For a practical first sample take the first 10 distinct relevant results across the queries, preserving order and noting any shortfall. Count unique independent exact-format creators, repeated uploads and large incumbents in that sample. Count results after removing reuploads and cross-posts of the same creative. For finalists expand to roughly 20 results if accessible. Log the most recent uploads and observed cadence on at least two direct competitors if available. Inspect the opening and payoff, or reliable visible evidence such as a creator transcript, before judging pacing, clarity or proof. Describe something testable, e.g. “three of six demos cut away before showing the dial.” A title or unseen clip cannot verify editing quality. Identify what someone does well, too. The sample is a lower bound on discoverable supply. A small search result set by itself is weak evidence of demand or scarcity.

**3 · Spot outliers (02:13–02:46).** For each relevant observation record the fields below. Divide views by subscribers or followers **on the same platform**. Use displayed approximate counts as approximate. A displayed 140K views / 28K subscribers is about 5×. If either figure is hidden, zero, from an unclear period or unavailable, mark the ratio unverified. If subscriber count is current, say so. Growth since publication distorts the ratio. Compare a post with nearby ordinary uploads at similar ages, excluding the breakout itself. If available, compare median views for the same format on the same account. Mark a same-account multiple separately. Do not multiply a page's unexplained “outlier” badge by a subscriber count.

**Working defaults, all author additions.** Triage accounts with up to 100K followers and posts at or above 2× their current account size. This broad screen catches posts that travelled beyond an existing following. A practical repeatability screen is three qualifying posts in 90 days across at least two independent small accounts in the target cohort. These are research settings, never Victor's thresholds or an empirical probability of success. Compare the local baseline. If mature accounts routinely clear 2× on Shorts or if 100K would dominate a small-language niche, set a more discriminating local screen, explain it from comparables and rerun consistently. Show ordinary and failed examples. Distinguish no qualifying records from no usable counts. Avoid treating a recent subscriber count paired with a years-old video as current proof.

**4 · Choose how to enter (02:46–07:49).** Pick an approach that the evidence supports.

| Choice | Test before selection | What to propose |
|---|---|---|
| Improve the format | Repeated small-account demand plus a specific weakness relative to the viewer's preference. Look for proof in the clips or specific viewer requests. | One visible improvement, e.g. let viewers see the component move before the explanation. |
| Transfer a proven mechanic | Check multiple independent source examples. Then search the destination in its own language. Identify audience interest and the weakness in current destination supply. | Preserve the underlying question and reveal. Change subject, evidence, casting, script and look. Strong origin + untested destination is a pilot hypothesis. |
| Scale through creators | The creator already has evidence of a working format and can coordinate contributors. Inspect distribution of hits, median results, costs and time. | Create an episode brief with space for original examples, creator notes, a review step and separate pay/rights per contributor. Continue tests of new formats, retire declining ones. |

**5 · Check whether the window is closing (07:49–08:53).** Compare this window with the preceding equal-length window. Match cohorts by time since posting where possible. Record changes in observed independent producers, the share occupied by large accounts, cadence and typical views per video. Views accumulate, so an older post's lifetime total is a poor baseline for a newer one. A meaningful fall alongside rising supply weakens the case. If comparable data is missing, durability is uncertain. Evaluate barriers such as unusual expertise or first-hand access and whether the user actually has them.

## Evidence ledger

Keep one row per post, plus a channel record linked by platform and account ID. Use direct URLs and dates. Shared sources can serve several rows only when their roles are stated.

| Field | Capture instruction |
|---|---|
| Context | Candidate, origin or destination, viewer, platform/surface, language/region, exact/adjacent/control, and how you found it. |
| Post | Title, author, direct URL, published date, time since posting, public views and date checked. For relative dates, preserve the displayed wording and mark approximations. |
| Account | Direct profile, followers/subscribers at observation, channel creation or join date, age as of check, public upload count. Earliest visible upload gives a lower bound on activity only. |
| Comparisons | Views ÷ followers, baseline median, denominator source and date, comparable ordinary post, reason included, independent account ID. |
| Supply and execution | Exact-format creators / results inspected, observed recent cadence, opening, payoff, gap and one competing explanation. Distinguish overlap across queries. |
| Provenance | Native page, official API, creator disclosure, or indexed snapshot with observation date if known. If a cached page has no observation date label it “snapshot date unverified.” Recheck near-cutoff metrics directly before calling a format qualified. |

YouTube watch pages and channel About or More info are convenient first choices. If an authorized Data API client is available, use video publication date and view count, then channel creation date, public video count and subscriber count. Preserve rounding and hidden counts. Check the definition of Shorts views for the observation period. On TikTok and Reels use the native post and profile. Keep views, plays, likes and engagement separate. Third-party estimates need a named source and date, and never replace a missing native figure silently. [YouTube video fields](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos) · [YouTube channel fields](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/channels)

## Monetisation, production and risk

- **Revenue.** Search the target geography, language and surface with terms such as `creator income report YouTube Studio RPM history long form` and `fitness Shorts RPM creator analytics country month`. Prefer a creator's disclosed revenue and eligible view denominator for the same period or the user's own analytics. Record currency, period, format, geography and source. Distinguish per-thousand creator revenue (RPM) from per-thousand advertiser impression cost (CPM). Compare like-for-like sources and give the observed range with limitations. With only one comparable give that observation; with none report “RPM range unverified.” Shorts RPM uses engaged views. Do not infer RPM from public views, CPM or estimated channel earnings. Search direct sponsor disclosures or “paid partnership” posts and comparable products. Give sponsor category, evidence of actual spend if found, and a product the user can deliver. Distinguish category fit from a signed sponsor. [YouTube revenue definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314357)
- **Resources.** Use the profile's equipment, permissions, hours, editing level and budget. Price necessary tools from official pricing pages for the user's country, including subscription minimums and usage limits. Estimate recurring spend and hours per finished video, amortizing fixed costs over the actual upload plan. Show the first-month cash outlay separately. Adjust scope or drop the candidate if it exceeds a hard limit.
- **Rules and rights.** Look up the current target platform's rules in the user's region and note the check date. Assess originality, reused footage, repetitive mass production, synthetic-media disclosure and rights to media, music and voice. Use primary sources when checking health or financial claims and account for the user's actual qualifications. For a synthetic reconstruction, label what was reconstructed where viewers see it. Record source/license, attribution and changes for each third-party asset. Give a concrete risk and mitigation per finalist. [YouTube monetisation policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392) · [Altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491) · [Copyright on YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797466) · [TikTok guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/) · [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/)
- **Evidence limits.** Every outside assertion needs an identifiable source and check date. Creative titles and work estimates can cite the confirmed profile, source observation or explicit planning assumption. Mark missing numbers “unverified.” If no web access is available, stop before live findings, scores or recommendations. Partial access supports partial findings. Include the terms tried and the missing evidence that would change the decision.

## Thirty-day decision

Budget the research, shoot/edit, publish and measurement work. For the chosen format, test a single change at a time with different original episodes. Log age-matched views, views/followers if defined, retention at the reveal, and a goal-specific result such as qualified enquiries. Use impressions click-through rate where the platform reports it for the surface. For a Shorts feed, examine stayed-to-watch and engaged-view retention, and report thumbnail CTR only on applicable impressions. Compare with the creator's own same-format baseline. Revise the opening if sampled viewers consistently leave before the promise, or revise the payoff if they leave before the reveal. Where exposure is too small, mark the test inconclusive. Recheck competitor supply before scaling.
