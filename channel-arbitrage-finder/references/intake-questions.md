# Intake interview

**Addition A1.** The video defines competition at the format level and asks what its audience prefers. These questions supply the creator constraints it leaves open. Use the answers to define a specific audience, an episode the creator could repeat, and a viewer payoff. See [Method Brief, §§3 and 9](method.md).

## How to conduct the interview

Greet the user in one line, then ask the first group. Wait for their answer after each group. Keep each turn to two to four short questions, apart from the final confirmation. Carry forward facts already supplied. If someone has volunteered a mode, explicitly ask them to confirm it, such as “You mentioned staying off camera and using AI visuals. Is faceless + AI the mode you want me to research?” Combine that with another relevant question. Allow “unsure,” a range, or “prefer not to say.” Record the uncertainty and its effect on research. Never infer a country or budget from a language.

If the user brings a saved profile, read it first. Ask “Is your saved production mode still right?” and “What has changed about your time, budget, goals or interests?” Retrieve missing fields in small groups, then use the confirmation below. A confirmed, complete profile is enough to resume research.

## Opening group · creative direction

“Let’s find a repeatable channel idea you can actually make.”

1. Which subjects or hobbies could keep you curious for 100 videos?
2. What do you do, know or have access to that other creators might find hard to reproduce?
3. Is there a niche or specific channel idea you want tested first?
4. Which topics, tactics or kinds of content are off limits?

If the user names an idea, ask what a typical episode would show and who would watch it. If they have no idea, pick two of their interests and ask which questions people already bring to them about those subjects. Combine this follow-up with the next group, splitting the turn if it would exceed four questions.

## Audience and goals

1. Which languages can you deliver confidently, and which would you like to publish in?
2. What country or region are you creating from, and which audience region should I research?
3. Which goals matter most to you from ad revenue, sponsorships, product or service sales, and building a personal brand? If you want a mix, which comes first?
4. Are you aiming for side income or a full business, and when would you like to see the first meaningful result?

For a seller, follow up on the offer and whom it helps. For a sponsor goal, ask which types of brands fit their audience. For a brand goal, ask what action would signal trust, such as a qualified inquiry. For ads, ask whether long-form or short-form best suits what they want to make. Bundle the relevant follow-up with the next group. Treat the timeline as an expectation.

## Platform and required mode choice

1. Which platforms should get the first episodes (YouTube long-form, YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels, or several)? If several, which is the priority?
2. Which production mode should we plan around? Choose **faceless + AI** (generated images, video or voice), **faceless, human-made** (your narration, screen recordings, animation, stock or hands-only footage), **on-camera**, or **hybrid**. What will viewers see and hear?
3. Which channels do you admire or dislike, and what specifically earns or loses your attention in an episode?

For several platforms, ask whether they can make native edits or only one cut. If they are torn between modes, describe an episode from their proposed niche in each of the two modes and ask which they would actually finish. Keep a tentative mode visibly provisional until they choose. A face-dependent concept needs an explicit yes to appearing in that segment.

## Ask the matching branch

| Selected mode | Questions for the next turn |
|---|---|
| Faceless + AI | Which image, video, editing or voice tools do you already have or pay for? Which visuals or voices do you want generated and which can you supply yourself? Would you spend more on a tool or on generation credits, and what monthly ceiling feels comfortable? |
| Faceless, human-made | Which tools, asset subscriptions or recording gear do you already use or pay for? Can you record your own voice, hands, screen or original footage? Which of those could you repeat weekly, and would you pay for more tools or assets? |
| On-camera | What phone or camera, lights, microphone and filming space do you have now? Would you rather speak unscripted, work from bullet points or read a script? Which demonstrations, props or locations could you film with that setup? |
| Hybrid | Which segments can show your face, and which need voice-over, footage, animation or AI? What phone or camera, lighting, microphone and space do you have, and how comfortable are you speaking unscripted? Which off-camera tools do you own or already pay for? Would you pay for more tools or credits? |

## Resources and feasibility

1. How many hours can you reliably spend each week, including research, filming, editing and publishing?
2. What is your monthly **total** budget for tools, people and assets, in which currency? How much of it is already committed?
3. How would you describe your editing experience (none, basic or advanced)? Which tasks can you finish alone?
4. How many videos per week could you keep publishing after the first month?

If the time, cadence and concept conflict, offer two concrete versions and ask which constraint can move. For example, a two-hour budget may support one hands-only demonstration with a single setup. If the creator still prefers a more demanding format, record it as a future option. If a tool price or market is unknown, preserve “unverified” for research.

## Confirm and save

Show six short lines on one screen. Fill them in from the conversation.

1. Interests, skills, professional experience and firm exclusions.
2. Niche to test first, sample audience and channel likes/dislikes with reasons.
3. Fluent and publishing languages, creator region and audience region.
4. Primary goal, other goals, ambition, timeline and target platforms.
5. Chosen mode, gear, space, speaking preference, available tools and willingness to add tools.
6. Weekly hours and uploads, monthly budget and existing commitments, editing skill and any open questions.

Ask “Is this accurate, or what should I change before I research?” Apply corrections, then save the confirmed values with [the user profile template](../templates/user-profile.md). Use a user-accessible location outside the installed skill so different creators retain separate profiles. In a chat without file writing, return the complete profile as copyable Markdown and tell the user how to bring it back. Begin with the nominated niche when one was supplied. Otherwise generate candidate formats from the confirmed interests and strengths.
