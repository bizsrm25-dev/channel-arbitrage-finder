## 1. The core idea in one sentence

Arbitrage means finding a repeatable video format that more people want to watch than creators currently make well, so a new entrant can earn unusually high attention per video while the gap lasts. The scarce item is a satisfying video in that format. The return is views per upload. There is no purchase price, resale transaction or promised cash return in this model.

Victor calls it “a format where there’s high demand and low supply” (01:50–01:57) and says to “look for formats where small accounts keep going viral” (02:39–02:46). The slides put the unit of analysis succinctly at 01:04, “Niches are industries. Formats are markets.” At 02:13 the practical cue is “Look for formats where small accounts keep breaking out.”

## 2. Why the gap exists

At 00:33–01:04, creators supply videos and viewers supply attention. The graph calls views per video the “price.” Two AI tutorials compete directly when they serve the same viewing occasion. AI news or a launch can draw away the same viewer as a substitute. A broad subject can therefore look crowded while one specific format remains underserved. Niche size sets a ceiling in his model. At 01:27–01:39 he contrasts millions of views for a strong sports video with perhaps 10,000 for AI business-to-business software. Both could be good results in their markets.

At 01:48–02:13, demand moves outward while relatively few people are making the format well. At 07:49–08:21, successful examples become visible, attract imitators and increase supply. The graph shows views per video falling as creator count rises. It gives no calendar for when that will happen. He urges speed and gives an exception at 08:08–08:21. When few people can execute a demanding format, the skill required can keep supply low.

## 6. Validation before committing

The explicit diagnostic is a format in which small accounts repeatedly go viral relative to their following (02:13–02:46). Use the format as the comparison unit. Look for multiple small-account breakouts, check what viewers reward, and decide whether your improvement or cross-niche transfer gives those viewers a concrete reason to watch. Established success elsewhere supports the mechanic. It leaves demand in the destination to be tested. A large creator's million-view video alone cannot distinguish audience size from format strength. Reassess the quality and quantity of competing supply as others catch on. The video offers no formal pilot protocol.

## 7. Production approach

The presenter speaks on camera and then appears in a webcam inset over slides. Case clips show real people, captions, graphics and footage. His quality criterion is the target viewer's preference. He explicitly allows simpler, more complex, more emotional, funnier or more highly edited videos. He describes writing scripts and giving creators notes. He does not specify whether the voice must be human or whether a creator must show their face. There is no prescribed studio layout, faceless workflow, synthetic voice, AI image/video generator, voice-over recipe or art style. The skill's four production paths are implementation additions. The video offers no evidence that faceless or AI-made production confers a distribution advantage.

## 8. Warnings and mistakes

- A broad niche hides the particular formats that compete directly. Raw view counts can also conceal how much reach a creator already has.
- A single hit is weaker evidence than recurring small-account breakouts. Budget and visual polish alone do not establish viewer preference.
- A proven mechanic still needs good execution in its destination niche. Scaling increases attempts and permits misses. A typical post need not resemble the campaign's largest hit.
- A solo creator’s time limits volume. Delegating execution introduces quality variation and can reduce the hit rate. He acknowledges cost differences and argues that deals or startup resources can make them worthwhile. He gives no universal cost advantage. Retire fading formats.
- Once the format's success is obvious, imitators can erode the opportunity. Deep niche knowledge and execution still matter after finding the gap.
