# Visual style library

**Addition A5.** Start with the part of the episode the audience needs to see. Choose a style that makes it legible with the creator’s actual tools and time. Victor’s quality principle at 02:56–03:12 leaves this choice to the audience and format. [Method Brief, §§3, 7 and 9](method.md)

Each reference frame below is an original scene you can stage, draw or generate. The quoted prompts also work as shot briefs for human production. In an AI report, adapt the selected style to three different beats of the actual episode. Deliver all three prompts fully written, along with a palette, narration direction and verified tool costs. Human-made branches use the physical or editing tools indicated. Put final text, dates and factual labels in the edit.

## 1. Paper-cutaway animation

**Visual reference** — Tear a slot in a sheet of cream card and slide a rust-red paper strip through it. Offset the layers enough to cast a narrow shadow. Suits everyday mechanisms, invention history and children’s science. Production modes include faceless + AI, faceless human-made, hybrid. Make it with paper and phone, Krita layers, or an image generator.

> Create a vertical overhead frame of a hand-cut paper loom. Three cream warp strips cross one terracotta weft strip on a dark teal backdrop. Lift one strip slightly so its shadow reveals the path. Soft window light, fibrous paper edges, quiet lower quarter for captions, no lettering.

## 2. Archival reading desk

**Visual reference** — Mount a verified scan beside a blank index card. Move from the whole page to one documented marginal detail. Suits local history, print culture and early computing. Production modes include faceless human-made, faceless + AI, hybrid. Make it with an original or cleared scan, GIMP, Shotcut. Check the rights on the individual item. [Library of Congress collection](https://www.loc.gov/free-to-use/)

> Create a horizontal overhead background plate for displaying a historic map in an edit. Empty charcoal desk, warm ivory mounting corners, blank index card at bottom right, diffuse light from camera left. Keep the entire mount empty so the editor can insert the actual sourced map. No written marks or invented documents.

## 3. Minimal vector explainer

**Visual reference** — Draw one package moving between three boxes. Highlight only the box responsible for the delay. Suits logistics, software concepts and basic economics. Production modes include faceless + AI, faceless human-made, hybrid. Make it with slide shapes, Krita or an image generator.

> Draw a 16:9 flat diagram on warm white. A blue parcel passes along three navy conveyor sections, with one amber gate lowered at the middle. Simple shapes and ample spacing, consistent stroke width, only one highlighted obstruction, no arrows with ambiguous direction, no type.

## 4. Cinematic 3D silhouette

**Visual reference** — Stand a neutral figure beside an oversized wheel. Use its silhouette to make scale readable. Suits design history, infrastructure and speculative explainers. Production modes include faceless + AI, hybrid, or faceless human-made with 3D skill. Make it with a 3D editor the user knows, or their licensed generator.

> Render a vertical museum-style study of an anonymous clay figure beside a railway wheel taller than the figure. Charcoal floor, slate-blue steel, thin amber rim light along the wheel, low eye-level camera, clear silhouettes, still background. Leave the upper third open. No brands, logos or lettering.

## 5. Exploded engineering plate

**Visual reference** — Lay out the body, spring and refill from your own click pen in assembly order. Frame perpendicular to the parts. Suits product teardown, mechanical history and repair education. Production modes include faceless human-made, faceless + AI illustration, hybrid. Make it with phone and desk, drawing layers or a 3D editor.

> Photograph or render a horizontal exploded view of a simple refillable pen, cap, barrel, metal spring and refill aligned along one axis in their real assembly order. Cream drafting paper, cool grey parts, orange spring, soft overhead light, one unoccupied margin for editor labels. Check the component order against the reference pen.

## 6. Screen-recording close-up

**Visual reference** — Show the entire editor once, then crop to the control the viewer needs. Hold on the actual before and after. Suits editing, spreadsheets and software tutorials. Production modes include faceless human-made, on-camera inserts, hybrid. Make it with OBS, the user's real application, Shotcut.

> Record your own blank practice spreadsheet in landscape. Show three unsorted names, click the sort control once, and hold the resulting order. Zoom into the control during the click, return to the identical starting frame size for the result. Use a blue pointer halo and no decorative cursor trails.

## 7. Locked comparison frame

**Visual reference** — Tape two squares on a bench. Shoot both outcomes from the same position so size and lighting can be compared. Suits crafts, product tests and coach-led demonstrations. Production modes include on-camera, faceless human-made, hybrid. Make it with phone, tripod or fixed support, editor split screen.

> Film two sheets of paper folded into different bridge shapes on the same pair of blocks. Match the angle, lighting, span and test load in both takes. Place the two real takes side by side, hold the moment each flexes, and leave room below for measured results. Warm grey bench with one yellow marker.

## 8. Tabletop stop motion

**Visual reference** — Advance six wooden tokens through three sorting trays under a fixed camera. A new position reveals each decision. Suits algorithms, game rules and manufacturing. Production modes include faceless human-made, hybrid, faceless + AI for a stylised illustration. Make it with phone, locked lighting, Shotcut image sequence or a generator.

> Create a five-second top-down animation on matte violet paper. Six pale wooden discs start in one line. Move one disc at a time into one of three shallow trays, snapping between poses like tabletop stop motion. Fixed camera, soft shadows, consistent object count, no lettering.

## 9. Macro object portrait

**Visual reference** — Rest an everyday object on black card and light it sideways until wear marks become visible. Suits collecting, material science and instrument care. Production modes include faceless human-made, on-camera inserts, hybrid. Make it with phone close-focus mode, desk lamp and black card.

> Film a close view of a used pencil eraser on black card, then slowly turn it under one side light so the rubbed surface catches the light. Cream rubber, warm grey debris, static background. Leave time before and after the turn for a clean cut. Show the object's actual surface.

## 10. Topographic relief map

**Visual reference** — Stack hand-cut contours into a hill and run a blue thread along the valley floor. Suits geography, transport and environmental history. Production modes include faceless + AI, faceless human-made, hybrid. Make it with card, thread, camera or layered drawing.

> Build a horizontal overhead relief model of an imaginary valley from stacked moss-green contour layers. A single blue thread follows the low ground while a cream road climbs a ridge. Raking light from the left reveals elevation. No town names or numerical elevations. Leave space at bottom right for a legend.

## 11. Chalk-talk diagram

**Visual reference** — Draw one mark per spoken instruction on a small dark board. Leave a visible trace of the earlier state. Suits maths, coaching tactics and logic puzzles. Production modes include on-camera, faceless human-made, faceless + AI, hybrid. Make it with chalkboard and phone, or Krita brush layers.

> Create a 16:9 chalkboard frame of a simple passing drill drawn in white. Three circles form a triangle, one yellow arrow connects two circles, and one dotted return path curves outside it. Dark green board, slight hand-drawn texture, empty right edge for the next addition, no text.

## 12. Field notebook

**Visual reference** — Place a real leaf beside a pencil drawing that singles out its branching veins. Suits nature observation, urban sketching and design research. Production modes include faceless human-made, faceless + AI, hybrid, on-camera inserts. Make it with sketchbook, phone, Krita or a generator.

> Illustrate a horizontal cream notebook spread. At left, one maple leaf seen from above. At right, a precise graphite study of its branching veins and one small muted-green wash sample. Diffuse outdoor light, faint paper tooth, generous empty margin for the editor's verified labels. No writing.

## 13. Projected silhouette theatre

**Visual reference** — Move a cardboard moon between a lamp and paper screen. Let the silhouette reveal the action. Suits astronomy, folk narrative and simple optics. Production modes include faceless human-made, faceless + AI, hybrid. Make it with paper screen, desk light and cutouts, or a generator.

> Make a six-second side-on silhouette animation of an imaginary eclipse demonstration. A small black disc glides across a pale gold illuminated circle on a dark indigo stage, then exits to reveal the full circle. Fixed camera, crisp edges, two shapes only, clear space at bottom for a narration caption.

## 14. Coach's replay

**Visual reference** — Film a real repetition from the side. Freeze at the point where the viewer can see the coaching cue and overlay one line. Suits fitness technique, music posture and sports instruction. Production modes include on-camera, hybrid. Make it with phone, stable support, editor freeze frame and drawing overlay.

> Film one unhurried bodyweight squat from the side in the coach's own studio, head and feet fully visible. Hold a neutral camera angle at hip height. Freeze once at the coach's selected teaching moment, add a single amber line for the cue, then resume the same take at normal speed. Keep overlay and captions clear of the movement.

## 15. Paired photographic contact sheet

**Visual reference** — Arrange four real stills of one restoration into a square so the viewer can inspect material changes. Suits restoration, cooking technique and craft experiments. Production modes include faceless human-made, on-camera inserts, hybrid, faceless + AI for an explicitly illustrated sequence. Make it with phone, GIMP or an image generator.

> Lay out a four-frame contact sheet showing the same wooden spoon throughout one documented refinishing session. Use your own photographs of its initial surface, sanding, wiping and completed finish. Match orientation, scale and soft side light. Use narrow cream gutters and charcoal surroundings. Place step labels later.

## 16. Isometric room model

**Visual reference** — Draw a small room with its roof and front wall removed. Highlight only the route or placement under discussion. Suits home studio layouts, architecture and workflow design. Production modes include faceless + AI, faceless human-made with drawing skill, hybrid. Make it with a simple 3D editor, Krita or an image generator.

> Create an isometric cutaway of a compact home recording room. One desk, one stool, camera on a low tripod, and one side window. Matt white walls, oak floor and a teal rug with a single amber accent at the camera position. Orthographic view, consistent furniture scale, no people or text, empty lower margin.

## Adapt the selected style

For a test episode, produce one opening image that makes the question visible, one explanatory view that changes what the viewer understands, and one reveal that delivers the promised result. Keep composition and palette consistent with the confirmed platform frame. Reduce the image to phone size and check whether the subject and change remain legible. For archived facts, physical tests, interface behaviour or movement technique, use verified evidence for the factual shot and reserve generated scenes for illustration.

Krita supports the hand-drawn styles, GIMP the still-image layouts, OBS the live screen captures and Shotcut the edit. Check the user's hardware, access and current terms before pricing a stack.
